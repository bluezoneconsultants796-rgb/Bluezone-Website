IELTS preparation: a week-by-week plan that works

Steady practice beats last-minute cramming. Pick your timeline, find your weak skill first and use official materials.

Start with a diagnostic

Take one full timed practice test under exam conditions before you plan anything. Mark Listening and Reading using the answer key. For Writing and Speaking, get feedback from a teacher, since self-marking is unreliable. Your lowest skill decides your plan because many universities set per-skill minimums.

Time depends on how much you study, your starting skills and your weakest section. These are planning guides, not guarantees.

Sample 8-week schedule

WeeksFocusWeekly routine
1–2FoundationsLearn the test format. Three Listening and two Reading sets. One Task 1 and one Task 2 with feedback. Record yourself answering Speaking Part 1 every day
3–4TechniqueQuestion-type strategy in Reading. Note-taking in Listening. Essay structure and paragraph planning. Speaking Part 2 practice with a timer
5–6Timed practiceTwo full sections per week under time. Two essays per week. Mock interview on Speaking Part 3 with a partner or teacher
7Full mockOne full mock test. Review every error by type, not just score
8PolishLight practice, sleep, logistics. No new strategies in the last two days

For a 4-week plan, compress weeks 1 to 2 into one week, weeks 3 to 6 into two weeks and keep the mock and polish. For a 12-week plan, add two weeks of grammar and vocabulary at the start and two more mocks.

Skill by skill

Listening

  • Read the questions before each recording plays
  • Practise spelling and plural forms, since marks are lost on small errors
  • Learn to follow signpost words that show a change of speaker or topic
  • Use the computer interface practice so the screen layout is familiar

Reading

  • Skim for structure, then scan for names, numbers and keywords
  • Do not spend more than about 20 minutes per passage
  • Practise True, False, Not Given carefully: False means the text says the opposite, Not Given means it does not say
  • Transfer answers carefully and watch the word limits

Writing

  • Plan for five minutes, write for 30, check for five in Task 2
  • Answer every part of the question, and give a clear position
  • Use paragraphs with one main idea each and linking that sounds natural
  • Spend about 20 minutes on Task 1 and 40 on Task 2, since Task 2 counts more

Speaking

  • Speak in full answers and extend with reasons and examples
  • Do not memorise scripts. Examiners notice and will probe
  • Practise describing people, places, events and experiences in 1 to 2 minutes
  • Record yourself and listen for fillers and repeated words

Free and official practice materials

  • British Council IELTS Ready Premium. Included with British Council Pakistan bookings: more than 40 practice tests, model answers and expert feedback, per the British Council booking page.
  • Computer familiarisation test and tutorial videos on the British Council IELTS site.
  • Cambridge IELTS books for official-style past papers.
  • Public Pakistani learning platforms such as the British Council’s free English resources.
Watch out: Avoid “leaked papers”, dumps and guaranteed-band courses. They do not work, and misconduct can lead to cancelled results and a ban.

Test-day checklist

  • Valid ID, the same as you booked with
  • Arrive early with your booking confirmation
  • Leave phones, watches and notes where instructed
  • Drink water and eat before the test
  • Attempt every question. There is no negative marking
  • Keep an eye on time per section, and do not stop at the end of Listening if you still need to transfer answers

Frequently asked questions

How long should I prepare for IELTS?

Most students who start near band 6.0 need 6 to 8 weeks of focused practice to gain half to one band. Starting at 5.0 or 5.5 may need 10 to 12 weeks. Progress depends on your weakest skill.

How can I improve IELTS Writing quickly?

Write to a timer, get feedback from a qualified teacher, and review repeated errors. Reading model answers helps only if you also write your own.

Is coaching necessary?

No. Many students prepare with official practice and a good study routine. Coaching helps most for Writing and Speaking feedback.

Can I retake just the weak section?

Yes, for computer tests, through One Skill Retake within 60 days if your institution accepts it.

What if my score is one band short?

Ask your university whether it accepts a lower score with a pre-sessional English course, request a remark if you believe marking was wrong, or retake. A remark cannot lower your score.
