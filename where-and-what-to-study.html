Build a shortlist of five to eight

  1. Set the budget ceilingTotal first-year cash from the table above, minus what you can genuinely fund.
  2. Check the entry rulesGrades, the 16-year degree question and the English score. See course advice.
  3. Sort into safe, match and reachTwo safe, three match, one or two reach. This protects you if one country tightens.
  4. Verify each providerUse the official registers below. A provider missing from its register cannot sponsor your visa.
  5. Compare the full costInclude tuition, rent, insurance, travel and the currency buffer.

Frequently asked questions

Should I choose the country first or the course first?

Choose the course and career goal first, then compare countries that teach it well within your budget. A strong course in a country you cannot afford is not a real option.

Which country is cheapest for Pakistani students?

Malaysia and China (especially with a CSC scholarship) usually cost least. Germany has low tuition at public universities but is not yet covered on this site. Compare the full first-year cost, not just tuition.

Do I need a 16-year degree for a master’s abroad?

Many universities outside the UK expect 16 years of education, meaning a four-year bachelor’s degree. Some accept a two-year BA or BSc plus a one-year bridging or postgraduate diploma. Check each university’s Pakistan entry page.

How many universities should I apply to?

Five to eight across at least two countries is a good balance. More than ten rarely improves results and costs fees and time.

Can you tell me which university will accept me?

Nobody can promise an offer. We can compare your profile with published entry rules and past admission patterns, then build a balanced shortlist.
